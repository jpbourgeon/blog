# About

<!-- Local portrait taken from the author's LinkedIn profile picture: acquire and approve the asset before publication. -->

My work sits at the intersection of public-sector management and software engineering. As technical director and deputy director of a public-sector IT department, I am interested both in how systems are designed and in the responsibilities of the people who operate them.

This blog is a personal space for reflection and sharing. I bring together ideas, practices, and technical work here, without speaking on behalf of my employer.

## Perspective

A system is more than its architecture or its features. It also shapes decisions, responsibilities, and people's ability to act. Whether the subject is software, an IT service, or an organization, I try to understand how these dimensions fit together—and where they come apart.

I am particularly interested in what it takes to remain in control: making choices explicit, verifying what we build, and preserving the ability to correct and change systems. These questions grow more urgent as we delegate more to tools and artificial intelligence.

For me, simplicity is not abstract minimalism. It is a design discipline: retaining only the complexity that is necessary, making trade-offs visible, and favoring what remains understandable and durable.

## Areas of exploration

**APE** explores management practices and the human experience of organizations: responsibilities, the tensions of everyday work, and the distance between decisions and what actually happens.

**42P** concerns the governance of what we produce and how it changes. Specification, rationale, verification, and a record of decisions help us retain control over the evolution of a system, especially when software agents take part in building it.

**STI — Sovereignty Through Institution** examines the conditions for human sovereignty in the face of artificial intelligence: what we delegate, where authority lies, and which institutions are needed to preserve control.

**FluidJ** is an application project focused on the governance of individual responsibilities. It explores ways to make commitments and responsibilities more explicit and manageable in everyday work.

**Cybernetic ITSM** (working name) approaches IT service management through both ITIL and a systemic, cybernetic perspective on organizations, their feedback loops, and how they are steered.

These areas connect without requiring every article to belong to a collection. Other subjects may find their place here when they contribute to the same inquiry.

## Projects and contributions

Some of these reflections lead to software and open-source contributions. They are a way to test architectural choices, technical trade-offs, and maintenance requirements that might otherwise remain abstract intentions.

My work can be found in particular under the [at-rama GitHub organization](https://github.com/at-rama). Repositories document the implementations themselves; articles on this blog explore their motivations, decisions, and limitations.
